# Charon

`shells/gnome` (installed as `gnome`): a desktop whose design follows GNOME Shell's, credited to the GNOME project; the code is Plutonium's. A top bar with Activities, the clock and quick settings; floating windows with edge tiling; dynamic workspaces, each one a thread; the Activities overview with a dash of pinned apps; and a Settings app. It's installed, off, like Styx; turn it on from the switcher or `plutonium run gnome`.

## What it does

- **Workspaces are threads.** Workspaces come and go as needed, as in GNOME. Each has its own tag, `gnome:workspace/<id>`: apps, tabs and Claude Code started on a workspace belong to its thread, and moving a window moves it to that thread. A workspace you rename (right-click its thumbnail) is kept even when empty, and its name becomes the thread's label. A moved window can still be reached from the thread it started in, because a window's first tag can't be dropped.
- **Overview.** Tapping Super, Alt+F1 or the top-left hot corner opens it; Super+A opens the app grid. Workspaces side by side as thumbnails to drag windows onto, window titles and close buttons, and search across apps, open windows, Settings pages and the web.
- **Laid out and animated as GNOME's** (`overviewControls.js`, `workspacesView.js`): the search entry, the thumbnails under it, then the workspace; the others a little smaller (0.94). In the app grid the workspaces shrink to a row (15% of the height) above the grid, windows where they are on the desktop, the thumbnails fade and shrink away and the grid comes up from below. Drag an app onto one of those workspaces to open it there; clicking one goes to it. Every state is a layout, and the view between two is their blend.
- **Animated as GNOME's, from a few eased values** rather than a CSS transition per element: the view (250 ms, ease-out-quad), the workspace scrolled to (a fractional index, 250 ms, ease-out-cubic, on the desktop with GNOME's 100px between workspaces), each workspace's place in the row, and per window its desktop rect, its spread rect (both in its workspace's own coordinates), hover, opening, minimizing and a pin. Each frame places everything from them, so a window is always its workspace's transform composed with its own: it stays on its backdrop while workspaces slide, in the overview, the app grid and the desktop alike, and its outline, caption and icon are drawn from the same rect. A swipe sets the values directly and picks up an animation where it is. A window moved with a switch stays put while the workspaces slide under it; one dropped on another workspace eases from where it was dropped; after a close in the overview the others wait 750 ms (WINDOW_REPOSITIONING_DELAY); an emptied workspace is removed once the scroll stops. The window under the pointer grows 5px a side over 200 ms, its outline (the window's own) fading in with it. Each window's app icon sits on its bottom edge and scales and fades with the overview (`windowPreview.js`), so it grows in as the windows spread and shrinks away into the app grid or the desktop.
- **Nothing is painted again while it animates.** Each frame changes only transforms, opacities and one clip, on elements that are layers of their own (`will-change`): the cards, the windows, the shade, the dash, the thumbnails, the captions' icons, the top bar's black. A card is its shadow and its wallpaper in a rounded clip, so its corners round with the view without the wallpaper's layer being rastered again; the shadow's corners change once a transition, to those of the state it's going to. Before (2026-11-07), the cards' corners and the top bar's colour were repainted each frame, and the desk, cards and windows were one screen-sized layer: on the CPU raster of a release on NixOS (no libpci, so no GPU raster, `lib/nixos.nix`) the overview ran at 20 to 30 fps on a 180 Hz display. After, 180 fps for opening and closing it, the app grid and switching workspaces, with or without windows.
- **The app grid is paged sideways, as GNOME's** (`iconGrid.js`): pages of 3×8, 4×6, 6×4 or 8×3, whichever is closest to the space's shape; dots under it and arrows at the sides; the wheel or a sideways swipe turns one page (300 ms, ease-out-cubic), and the arrow keys move onto the next page. It's built when the apps are listed and kept, and stays rastered while hidden (`will-change`), so opening it never shows tiles not drawn yet. Search results are a list, under the search entry.
- **Thumbnails show the windows themselves:** the broker's `snapshot()` of each window (or tab) drawn on a canvas, alpha and all, retaken every second while the overview is open and the page is shown. A snapshot doesn't resize the window or start a capture, so no capture indicator. A window's app icon stands in until its first one.
- **Dash.** The UI library's dock ([UI_LIBRARY.md](UI_LIBRARY.md), "The dock"), drawn as GNOME's: pinned apps and sites on Super+1…9, then running apps after a separator, then Show Apps. Pin from the right-click menu, by dragging an icon on, or with **+**; drag to reorder; drag off to unpin (with Undo). Clicks are Dash to Dock's (focus, then minimize, or list the windows). Super+Shift+N puts the focused window in slot N, Super+Ctrl+N opens it maximized, Super+Ctrl+Shift+N opens a new window. Web, Claude Code and Settings can be pinned. Settings → Apps lists the slots; Appearance has the dock's options and whether the dash shows all the time or at the bottom edge. Pinned apps from before the dock moved into its slots once.
- **Quick settings:** volume, microphone and brightness; Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Do Not Disturb, Keep Awake and Airplane Mode, each with a menu; the power menu. **Date menu:** notifications, what's playing, a calendar. Notification banners at the top.
- **Settings:** Network, Bluetooth, Sound, Power, Screen Lock, Appearance, Multitasking, Keyboard Shortcuts, Apps, Notifications, Date & Time, About.
- **Tiling.** Drag a window to the top edge to maximize, to a side for half the screen, to a corner for a quarter, with a preview. Super+arrows from the keyboard. Two tiled windows share their edge when one is resized.
- **Keys:** GNOME's defaults (Alt+Tab, Alt+\`, workspace switching and moving, Alt+F2, Alt+Space, Super+D, the screenshot keys). Every shortcut can be changed in Settings → Keyboard, and custom commands added.
- **Touchpad swipes** (the shell library's `touchpadswipe`, DRM only): three or four fingers sideways switch workspaces; up opens the overview, up again the app grid, down goes back. The view follows the fingers, one step a swipe.

## Things to know

- With "Super opens the overview" on, the shell takes every Super combination, so apps see none (niri does the same). It's a switch in Settings.
- A keyboard whose F-keys Plutonium treats as a Chromebook top row sends F1–F4 as Back, Forward, Refresh and Zoom; those are bound too. F5 and up become brightness and media keys there, so Alt+F7, F8 and F10 may not work on such keyboards.
- A browser tab dragged partly off the screen overflows like a native window (Chromium's embedded surfaces patch); before it, the tab laid out again to fit its visible part.
- A focused browser tab keeps the keyboard from the shell. To take it back the shell hides the tab for two frames.
- Storage keys must match `[\w.-]`; the per-session key is sanitized.
